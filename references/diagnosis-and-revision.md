# Diagnose and revise the actual problem

Use this reference for critique, a stuck draft, revision, continuation, or adaptation. Review the material actually supplied. If only an excerpt is available, qualify conclusions about the larger work.

## Find the cause

Record a useful finding in this form:

| Evidence | Audience effect | Likely cause | Repair | Consequences to recheck |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A specific passage, scene, choice, or omission | What becomes hard to follow, feel, anticipate, or believe | The underlying decision causing the effect | A concrete change at that level | Other passages or states affected by the change |

Use this table only when it helps comparison; a short paragraph is enough for one finding. Separate a demonstrated contradiction from a plausible weakness and an optional preference. Quote only the passage needed to locate the problem.

Prioritize the weakness that most undermines the user's purpose. Usually that means task fit, factual basis or continuity, narrative progression and character logic, then scene execution and language. Change the order when the request is specifically about another level.

## Common symptoms and targeted repairs

| Symptom | Inspect | Possible repair | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opening feels unengaging | What the audience can care about, notice, or wonder; orientation and voice | Start at a more revealing moment, sharpen the specific situation, or change the information order | Adding an unrelated attack or a sensational claim |
| Middle feels repetitive | Whether encounters change tactics, information, relationships, or interpretation | Merge repeated functions, alter consequences, or deepen a pattern | More obstacles that leave the situation unchanged |
| Character decision feels unearned | What the character knows, wants, believes, and can do at that point | Establish a motive, change the available evidence, or revise the decision | Explaining afterward that they acted because the plot needed it |
| Stakes feel remote | What can be lost, gained, understood, or preserved in concrete terms | Make the significance visible in behavior and circumstance | Inflating every story into a global catastrophe |
| Emotion feels asserted | What the moment establishes through attention, interaction, action, or reflection | Add or select the experience that makes the feeling intelligible | Multiplying emotion labels and bodily reactions |
| Dialogue feels generic | Speaker purpose, relationship, vocabulary, known information, and subtext | Revise lines around different intentions and responses | Giving everyone a quirky catchphrase |
| Mystery feels confusing | Which uncertainty is deliberate and whether the audience can follow the current situation | Clarify orientation while retaining the intended unknown | Revealing the answer merely to eliminate every question |
| Twist feels arbitrary | Prior evidence, established knowledge, and world rules | Prepare a fair alternate reading or change the twist | A late exception that erases earlier facts |
| Viewpoint feels inconsistent | Narrator role, focal character, knowledge, and transition markers | Externalize inaccessible information or mark an intentional shift | Flattening all perspectives into one voice |
| Pacing drags | Repeated functions, explanation, scene/summary balance, and attention | Compress low-value transitions or redistribute emphasis | Cutting every reflective or quiet passage |
| Ending feels unearned | Opening expectations, prior choices, unresolved threads, and intended closure | Revise preparation or the ending's connection to the established work | Adding a moral or epilogue by default |
| Quiet story feels inert | Selection, accumulation, recurrence, relation, and perceptual change | Make the organizing pattern more legible or particular | Forcing a villain or disaster |
| Worldbuilding overwhelms | What must be understood for the present action or experience | Reveal relevant constraints through use, perception, or brief summary | Withholding all orientation or dumping a glossary |
| Continuation contradicts canon | Existing facts, timeline, character knowledge, and relationship state | Correct the contradiction or establish the proposed change | Treating a new guess as an established fact |
| Interactive choice feels hollow | Advertised agency and persistent consequences | Change state, later recognition, or the choice's honest framing | Secretly erasing consequential decisions |
| Factual narrative feels thin | What evidence is available and what is missing | Research, attribute, summarize, or acknowledge the gap | Inventing dialogue, motives, or atmosphere |
| Adaptation feels like a transcription | Which functions the original medium carried | Recreate those functions with the new medium's resources | Preserving wording while losing the experience |

These are diagnostic hypotheses. Verify against the user's intention; a technique can be deliberate and effective even if it resembles one of these symptoms.

## Revision passes

Select only the passes relevant to the task:

1. **Purpose and structure:** Does the draft produce the intended experience? Make a reverse outline of what units actually do. Find missing links, duplicated functions, misplaced reveals, or a form that conflicts with the brief.
2. **People and relationships:** Check important decisions against motives, circumstances, and knowledge. Check who changes, who stays stable, and why relationships move.
3. **Scenes and information:** Check orientation, purpose, viewpoint, action-response links, dialogue, and distribution of scene and summary.
4. **Continuity and evidence:** Check chronology, rules, established facts, clue placement, quoted material, source support, and interactive state.
5. **Language and delivery:** Check voice, precision, rhythm, redundancy, format, length, and the medium's practical constraints.

A request to correct a few lines may need only pass five plus a quick continuity check. A factual contradiction may need pass four first. Structural revision and sentence editing have different purposes; do not polish scenes that the agreed revision removes.

Source: [George Mason University on revision and reverse outlining](https://writingcenter.gmu.edu/writing-resources/writing-as-process/revising). The staged fiction workflow above is this skill's adaptation, not a formal method attributed to that source.

## Preserve the user's work

Before revising, identify the facts, voice, relationships, ending constraints, and successful passages to retain. When the user explicitly requests reinvention, honor the broader scope rather than preserving everything mechanically.

Apply an authorized rewrite rather than listing suggestions indefinitely. If multiple repairs would materially change the intended story, make the alternatives concrete so the user can choose. For a reversible local decision, choose a fitting repair and explain it briefly if useful.

Work from the latest draft. For file edits, target the requested file and section, preserve unrelated changes, and keep a previous version when a substantial rewrite or the user's workflow requires recoverability. Creation is not permission to publish, send, or submit the work externally.

## Check the effects

After a repair, trace what it changes downstream. Moving a clue can change who knows it; changing a motive can invalidate later speech; compressing time can make travel impossible; changing an interactive flag can unlock an ending too early.

Read the revised unit in its surrounding context. Compare it with the actual brief, not an invented standard of professional writing. Check requested word or duration limits using an available tool when precision matters; label estimates honestly.

Deliver the revised artifact with a concise account of consequential changes when the user wants it. For critique-only tasks, provide prioritized findings and concrete options. For final-copy-only tasks, give only the requested copy.

Do not report an executed test, reader response, fact-check, or originality check unless it actually occurred. A self-assessment is useful but remains a self-assessment.
